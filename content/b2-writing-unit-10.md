answer: "The saying that necessity is the mother of invention contains real truth, but it is incomplete. || Needs certainly push people to act, because a difficult problem creates pressure to find a practical answer to everyday difficulties. Moreover, limited resources often force inventors to reuse materials in clever ways that nobody had considered before. However, necessity alone does not produce a new machine or idea; someone must first imagine a different possibility. On the other hand, pure imagination without a clear need may remain an amusing toy that never improves anyone's life. || Consequently, the most useful inventions usually join observation of a real problem with creative thinking. Furthermore, history shows that many breakthroughs were noticed only because someone questioned an accepted routine. In addition, persistence matters, since early designs rarely work perfectly, and several attempts may be needed before a useful version appears. Therefore, schools should teach both practical problem-solving and freedom to speculate. || Ultimately, invention grows where curiosity meets genuine human need and patient experiment."
modelAnswerZh: "「需要乃发明之母」这句谚语确有道理，但并不完整。 || 需求确实推动人们行动，因为一个难题会产生压力，要求为日常困难找到实际的答案。此外，有限的资源常常迫使发明者以前所未有的巧妙方式重复使用材料。然而，仅有需要并不会产生新机器或新想法；必须有人先想象出一种不同的可能。另一方面，没有明确需要的纯粹想象，可能只是一直不曾改善任何人生活的有趣玩具。 || 因此，最有用的发明通常把对真实问题的观察与创造性思维结合起来。此外，历史表明，许多突破之所以被注意到，只是因为有人质疑了一种被接受的常规。另外，坚持也很重要，因为早期设计很少能完美运作，在有用的版本出现之前可能需要多次尝试。因此，学校应既教授实际解决问题，也给予自由推测的空间。 || 归根结底，发明生长于好奇心与真实人类需求及耐心实验相遇之处。"
core: "Necessity creates pressure to solve everyday problems and inspire useful experiments, and scarce resources encourage inventive reuse. Yet need alone is not enough, since someone must imagine a different possibility; imagination without a real purpose stays a toy. Lasting inventions combine observation of genuine difficulties with creative thinking, a willingness to question routine and persistence through failed designs. Education should therefore join practical skills with free speculation about the future. Both forces are therefore essential."
