text: "Write this passage in English"
sentence: "Write about a teacher i admire in English. || Requirements: 3-4 paragraphs, 100-150 words || A Chinese structure guide (中文结构提示) is provided below — use it to structure your essay || Key vocabulary hints: teacher, admire, patient, mistake, courage, encourage"
answer: "The teacher I admire most is my English teacher, whose patience and warmth changed the way I see learning itself. || Never once have I seen her criticise a student for giving a wrong answer; instead, she gently guides us to discover the correct one for ourselves by asking the right questions. Her lessons are lively and full of encouragement, so even the shyest students gradually find their voice. acceptAlso: "My English teacher earns my deepest respect through her endless patience. || She never shames a wrong answer, preferring to lead us gently toward the right one, and she treats every error as a step forward. She showed me that great teachers offer not just facts but the confidence to use them. || I still use the questions she taught us to ask whenever I read something difficult, and they never fail to help."

answer: "Many students complain about homework, and I understand why, but I believe a reasonable amount of it is genuinely valuable. || The knowledge we meet in class does not settle in our minds overnight; we need to revisit it, practise it and use it again before it truly becomes ours. acceptAlso: "Although homework is unpopular, I find moderate amounts useful. || Class lessons need revisiting before they become lasting knowledge, and working alone at home trains time management. Excessive tasks, however, breed resentment and exhaustion. Balance is everything. Completing my work honestly became my first lesson in self-discipline. When the amount is fair, homework also shows parents what we are learning and gives them a real chance to help us. || I have found that starting it early makes the whole evening calmer."

answer: "I used to be terribly afraid of making mistakes, and a single wrong answer on a test could spoil my whole afternoon. || Then a teacher told me something I have never forgotten: mistakes are the best teachers we have, because they point straight at the gaps in our understanding. Gradually my results improved, but something else changed too. || I stopped being frightened of failure, because I came to see each mistake as useful information rather than proof of stupidity, and that attitude has helped me grow in ways good grades never could."

acceptAlso: "Fear of errors once held me back, until a teacher explained that mistakes reveal precisely what we do not understand. || I began recording my wrong answers and revisiting them until they no longer defeated me. My marks improved, but more importantly the fear disappeared. I now treat every slip as helpful feedback rather than a personal failure. || I now share my error notebook with classmates, and we often learn from one another's slips as much as our own."
srsWords: "mistake" "error" "notebook" "improve" "fear" "progress"

answer: "I was never very fond of group projects, because in my experience one or two people often did very little while the rest of the team carried the weight, and that felt deeply unfair. || Last term, however, a particular project changed my mind completely. acceptAlso: "Group work used to frustrate me, as lazy members left others carrying everything. || A project last term proved different: clear roles and regular check-ins kept everyone accountable and we excelled. I learned to value differing opinions and saw that true teamwork means each person contributes their unique strength. I also learned to appreciate quieter teammates, whose careful work often matters more than the loudest voice in the room. || Our teacher later praised not just the result but the way we shared responsibilities."
