answer: "Traditions give people a sense of belonging that is difficult to replace in modern life. || They connect individuals to their families, communities and history, creating a stable identity in a rapidly changing world. For example, a grandparent who tells stories in a regional dialect passes on humour and wisdom that translation cannot fully capture. When a language is neglected, younger speakers lose access to jokes, songs and family histories that carry emotional weight. Consequently, holidays can simplify history into a flattering national myth rather than an honest reflection. On the other hand, critical citizens can use the same occasion to ask difficult questions and include voices that were previously silenced. || Citizens can read speeches critically, compare competing accounts and discuss uncomfortable chapters with their children. A mature commemoration admits that pride and shame may coexist in the same national story. Only then does a public holiday become an occasion for thought rather than simple celebration. In my view, remembrance is valuable only when it accepts complexity. || Honest education about the past helps young people inherit responsibility without inheriting hatred."

answer: "Genuine cultural exchange depends on respect rather than simple curiosity. || When people encounter a foreign tradition, they often rely on stereotypes that reduce a rich culture to a few recognisable images. Moreover, modern media accelerate this problem by spreading simplified symbols far faster than accurate knowledge. Consequently, borrowing unfamiliar clothing, music or religious signs can become cultural appropriation, in which a dominant group profits from meanings it does not understand. On the other hand, exchange can be enriching when it is based on equal dialogue and honest acknowledgement of origins. || A visitor who learns a few words of the local language and asks about the meaning of a ceremony shows more respect than one who simply takes photographs. Such small gestures turn observation into a relationship. In my opinion, people should ask who benefits from a cultural practice and whether its original community has been consulted. True respect means listening as much as borrowing, and accepting that some traditions are not ours to use. || Museums and schools can present a culture from within, explaining its history instead of displaying it as exotic decoration."
modelAnswerZh: "真正的文化交流依赖于尊重，而不仅仅是好奇心。 || 当人们遇到一种外国传统时，常常依赖刻板印象，把丰富的文化简化为几个容易辨认的符号。此外，现代媒体加速了这一问题，因为简化的符号传播得远比准确的知识快。结果，借用陌生的服饰、音乐或宗教标志可能变成文化挪用，即主导群体从自己并不理解的意义中获利。另一方面，如果交流建立在平等对话和对来源的坦诚承认之上，它也可以丰富双方。 || 一个学会几句当地语言、并询问某个仪式含义的访客，比只顾拍照的人表现出更多尊重。这样的小举动把观察变成了关系。依我看，人们应当问：谁从某种文化实践中获益？它的原初社群是否被征求过意见？真正的尊重意味着倾听与借用同样多，并接受有些传统并不属于我们，不能随意使用。 || 博物馆和学校可以从内部呈现一种文化，解释其历史，而不是把它当作异国情调的装饰来展示。"

Borrowing symbols without understanding can become appropriation when powerful groups profit from unfamiliar meanings. However, equal dialogue and acknowledgement of origins make exchange enriching. Education is crucial because it reveals power differences and challenges comfortable assumptions. Curiosity without humility easily becomes exploitation."
