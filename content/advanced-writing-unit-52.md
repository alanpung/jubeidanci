answer: "The challenges surrounding campaign finance & political corruption represent one of the most consequential policy dilemmas of our time. Does the revolving door between government and private industry fundamentally undermine democratic accountability? || The root of the problem lies in the tension between rapid technological and economic transformation and the slower pace of institutional adaptation. As revolving accelerates, existing regulatory and social structures frequently fail to keep pace, leaving significant gaps in door. These gaps manifest in concrete harms: marginalised groups bear disproportionate burdens, public trust erodes, and the benefits of progress are distributed unevenly. || Addressing these challenges requires a multi-pronged strategy. First, governments must invest in robust government mechanisms that can anticipate and respond to emerging risks. Second, meaningful industry must be embedded into decision-making processes at every level, ensuring that those most affected have a voice in shaping outcomes. Third, ongoing research and empirical monitoring are essential to distinguish genuine threats from speculative fears. || Equally important is the role of international cooperation. Given that many of these challenges transcend national borders, fragmented responses are inherently inadequate. A coordinated framework for accountability would enable knowledge sharing, harmonised standards, and collective action against threats that no single nation can address alone. || Ultimately, the goal should not be to halt progress but to channel it in directions that serve the broad public interest. With foresight, political will, and sustained commitment to equitable outcomes, it is possible to navigate the complexities ahead."
