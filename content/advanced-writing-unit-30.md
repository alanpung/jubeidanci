Moreover, they point to the welfare benefits that current approaches have delivered, suggesting that disruption could produce unintended consequences. || Conversely, critics maintain that reliance on self-regulation is insufficient. Furthermore, they argue that the pace of change has outstripped the capacity of existing institutions to respond effectively, creating a dangerous governance gap that demands immediate action. || A balanced resolution requires acknowledging the legitimacy of both perspectives. As break-up accelerates, existing regulatory and social structures frequently fail to keep pace, leaving significant gaps in remedies. First, governments must invest in robust competition mechanisms that can anticipate and respond to emerging risks. A coordinated framework for benefit would enable knowledge sharing, harmonised standards, and collective action against threats that no single nation can address alone. || Ultimately, the goal should not be to halt progress but to channel it in directions that serve the broad public interest.
